# Connections that explain and remain editable

Read when laying out a framework/mechanism with conditioning, training or return paths, and when reconstructing its native connections. This document owns relation endpoints, routing and connection behavior. Scientific relations come from illustration-design.md; contour and arrow appearance come from shape-vocabulary.md.

## Define the relation before the stroke

Select the visible relations from the figure's reader question. A paper illustration explains a contribution; it need not draw every argument of every function. Keep the principal route and the few relations that explain the new operation. Expand an update/conditioning mechanism in a local inset when its full wiring overwhelms the overview. Account for omitted preparation or repeated calls in the caption/detail rather than adding global arrows. Dense useful mechanisms can coexist with sparse, legible connections.

For each shown important relation, identify its producer, consumed quantity, receiving operation and phase. A short note in the brief is sufficient; no universal graph schema is required. Distinguish computation, conditioning, parameter updates, state returns and shared-parameter associations. A route around the picture is not evidence of a feedback loop.

Attach the relation to the appropriate object or port. Input tokens enter the consuming operation; a clock embedding enters its conditioning interface; an objective updates parameters; a sampler's updated state returns to the next input. Do not attach a state-return path to an enclosing panel, shadow or held-state row merely because its border is convenient. Show a local output carrier or labeled port when the true producer would otherwise be unclear.

Store port ownership in the editable file without displaying every anchor as a circle or variable tile. A visible junction communicates a branch; a connection point is usually an implementation detail. Separate these roles. A labeled bundle can abbreviate several related inputs, provided it does not imply an unsupported combining operation. A single generic iteration route can refer to the selected active stream when compact mode cases explain what changes; separate global loops are useful only when they explain genuinely different processes.

A concise connection label names the transmitted quantity or operation, not an entire claim. Keep carrier properties separate from roles: an unchanged state, a clean condition and a sampled output may look similar but participate in different relations.

When a feedback bundle branches, each consumer receives quantities actually present in that producer. A narrower state/history carrier cannot introduce an absent sensing modality through a new outgoing label; route that modality from its real observation producer. In a compact teaser, an adaptation/correction cue also needs a visible effect on the next decision or command, not only an incoming observation route ending at the optional block.

## Ports and route space are layout decisions

Choose source and target sides before filling the diagram with motifs. Main computation usually has a short forward path; conditioning can enter a different side; training and returns need reserved corridors. These choices follow the actual dependencies. Draw a return from its output producer first, then choose the loop's corridor and input terminal. If a long route cannot fit legibly, move a supporting region or use a labeled continuation pair; do not force it through text or run it against the page edge.

On a roughly 1600px-wide plate, 18–28px is a useful initial separation between neighboring route centerlines or a route and unrelated content. Keep a visible gutter beyond the route's outer stroke and arrowhead to the crop boundary. Adjust after inspecting the final physical size. Large edge-following loops must not look like another panel frame. Preserve the compact accepted layout while opening only the corridors that fail.

Arrow tips end at the receiving object's visible boundary or a defined port. Use the body's geometry, not its shadow or a group's union bounding box. The source touches its producer. Separate incoming relations into readable ports instead of stacking arrowheads. Give the final approach enough length for the head; a large head on a tiny shaft can overlap its source or appear to point backwards. Use clean orthogonal turns rather than corrective kinks.

### Align bodies, carriers and terminals together

Establish local row/column guides for a related mechanism before drawing its connections. Equal-role modules share a baseline or centerline; token strips share cell centers and spacing; parallel branches use consistent stage positions. Align a photo board by its visible crop and caption band, not the original image's transparent margin. A dense asymmetric composition can have several local guides; it does not need one grid imposed on every object.

Carry a branch's guide through a shared module when its internal carrier represents that same stream. An aligned external port with an offset internal token row still suggests the wrong correspondence. Reserve each row's heading/caption band before packing the next row; remove a redundant caption rather than allowing it to collide with the next branch's title.

Choose the terminal from the interface's meaning, then align its geometry. A single input usually enters the center of the receiving face, and its producer leaves the corresponding face center. Use the center of the visible side segment for a trapezoid, not the center of its enclosing rectangle. A token carrier's port sits at the token row's center; a sum's terminal lies on its circle. Distinct inputs may require separate named or implicit ports on one face: space them evenly around the face center when their roles are equal, or align each with its actual internal consumer when they differ. Do not collapse them onto a center point merely to make the picture symmetric.

For short neighboring connections, align the two terminals to a common horizontal or vertical guide when a small local adjustment leaves module ownership and internal layout intact. Prefer moving a carrier or choosing a valid point on a broad receiving face over moving an entire accepted region. If exact alignment would break those relationships, open a real routing corridor or revise the local arrangement; neither a diagonal shortcut nor a tiny corrective elbow solves the layout. Align the final approach with the intended receiving face when a routed connection needs a perpendicular arrival.

Parallel routed inputs share deliberate bend columns or rows with separate arrival ports and enough separation between shafts and heads. A fork has one visible common stem and a junction aligned with its outgoing routes; repeated relations must not create slightly displaced duplicate stems. A merge aligns to the combining operation, not an arbitrary point between its inputs. Center a quantity label over the readable shaft segment it describes, offset it from the line, and leave the tip unobstructed. Keep return/update corridors independent of the main row guides.

Use these guides in the master and native reconstruction alike. Store the resolved endpoint/waypoint coordinates and attachment intent in task-local authoring data. Application snapping to a group box, stroke shadow or transparent image margin can undo the intended alignment; inspect the visible bodies and actual native arrowheads at final size. Correct accidental offsets without regularizing intentional sketch contours, photographs or scientific data coordinates.

Conceptual connections default to horizontal or vertical shafts. A straight connection requires aligned terminals, not merely nearly equal height. Reserve orthogonal elbows for meaningful row changes, obstacles, separated arrivals and return corridors. Separate parallel routes and their final approaches; give a fork a readable common stem instead of a fan of diagonals. A diagonal or curved conceptual arrow requires an explicit requested composition or scientific spatial meaning, rather than convenience.

This rule governs module relations, not scientific coordinates or texture. Preserve real slopes in numerical curves, trajectories, physical rays and geometric constructions. Keep diagonal hatching and irregular contours where they carry the sketch style. Do not convert measured lines into staircase plots or flatten geometry to satisfy diagram alignment.

Lay out the main route first, give state feedback its own return corridor, and keep training relations in a local inset or separate band. Prefer a few clean bends over the shortest geometric route; if that route needs repeated corrective bends, move its module or reduce the overview's visible relations. Apply the same waypoints in native exports and inspect their actual bends. Routing is part of the composition, not a post-export attempt to connect already packed boxes.

When correcting an arrow's producer or consumer, recompute its entire corridor. Changing only the endpoint can leave a newly correct relation crossing intervening modules. If a necessary detour needs more than a simple three-segment elbow, use a supported multi-bend native connector with one owned head, or adjust the local arrangement. Do not force the route through labels to fit the exporter's first connector preset. A shaft must also clear the producer's contour: avoid a hidden horizontal segment running along its edge before the visible arrow leaves.

## Junctions, crossings and direction

A branch denotes reuse of the same quantity; show a small junction dot when that distinction matters. A merge needs a real combining/selecting operation when the quantities differ. A crossed route without a junction is not a merge: leave a visible gap/bridge or reroute one path. Use no dot at a mere crossing. Keep diagonal hatch, contour strokes and arrow shafts visually separate.

One directed route has its head at the receiver. Bidirectional exchange uses a deliberate double-headed relation or separated directions, not two overlapping single arrows with unreadable tips. Shared weights, zoom links and group brackets are associations and normally have no computation head. A stop-gradient mark crosses the actual gradient path; freezing a module does not mean blocking every data path through it.

When sampling is conditional, expose which update is active and which state is held. Branches leaving a common updated-state port can be labeled with their actual condition. A return of state and an update of a timestep are related operations, not interchangeable quantities. Keep noise/sampling clocks distinct from physical time.

## Native relation objects

Use an ordinary native arrow as the default: one continuous straight or elbow connector (curves only for the exceptions above) with its own receiving-end arrowhead. A routed arrow remains one owned relation, including its bends, dash pattern and head. Do not build normal arrows from separate short shafts and a standalone triangle. That construction introduces visible joints, inconsistent heads and independent movement; it is not a substitute for a connector merely because every part is editable. Sketch character belongs primarily to the module contours and designed layers; keep connectors smooth and deliberate, with consistent shaft weight and moderate built-in heads.

In draw.io use one native edge with its native end arrow and editable waypoints. An edge with only sourcePoint/targetPoint is a positioned line; attaching source/target cells and explicit entry/exit locations makes the relationship editable with its modules. Use fixed ports when the receiving interface or an aligned row matters. Floating perimeter attachment is appropriate only when a side may change without changing meaning; inspect it for automatic off-axis terminal placement. Explicit entry/exit positions on the actual body can preserve an orthogonal approach that default perimeter snapping would skew. Keep waypoints in the edge's parent coordinate system. Avoid putting an external relation inside one endpoint's moving group. In SVG use one path with an intrinsic marker-end rather than an independently positioned head.

In PPTX use the application's/native library's connector shape and line-end decoration, with actual attachments to supported bodies/submodules. If an arrowhead is reversed, repair the connector orientation/flip or its correct line-end setting. Do not replace all heads with loose polygons to bypass that bug. Choose a simple supported connector geometry before adding custom routing; a small layout adjustment often preserves the explanation better than a fragmented exact trace. A grouped freeform is not automatically a reliable connection site. Saved connection IDs alone do not prove live behavior: an importer can convert even an ungrouped native connector into an ordinary custom shape. Inspect the authoring tool's supported sites and the actual application before claiming attachment; use verified sites or disclose the application's manual-routing limitation. Do not infer one application's live behavior from another's rendering or its round-tripped file.

For an unusually important path whose geometry the tool cannot represent, prefer one continuous editable path with an owned line-end decoration and explicitly disclose its attachment/rerouting limit. A detached-head or segmented approximation is a last resort for that identified path, never the figure-wide default. Keep it visibly coherent and report the affected relation. Shape editability, connector continuity and live attachment are different deliverable properties.

## Inspect the delivered connections

Read the actual native render from producer to consumer. Confirm visible tip direction, terminal contact, junction meaning, label clearance and the quantity returned by each loop. Repeated arrows in token strips also need an unambiguous direction; a correct graph elsewhere does not fix them.

Move one connected styled module on a copy of the current delivered file, then inspect/save/render it in the native application. Check that attached terminals follow and that route bends and arrowheads remain understandable. If only endpoint metadata is verified while the application has not rerouted a moved shape, state that narrower result. Correct an observed failure; do not repeat a full diagram audit after an unrelated label edit. Preserve the prior artifact when making a wiring correction.
